# 📚 Introduction

Data has become an important resource in modern technology. Organizations collect and analyze personal information for purposes such as providing services, improving products, conducting research, and making decisions.

However, the collection and use of personal information can create ethical concerns. Even when data collection is technically legal, organizations should consider whether the collection and use of data is **fair, transparent, necessary, and respectful of individual rights**.

Data ethics provides a framework for making responsible decisions about how data is collected, processed, analyzed, stored, and shared.

---

# 🧠 Major Ethical Principles

Several principles are important when handling personal data:

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🔒 **Privacy** | Respect individuals' control over their personal information. |
| 🎯 **Purpose Limitation** | Use data for clearly defined and appropriate purposes. |
| 📉 **Data Minimization** | Collect only the information that is necessary. |
| 👁️ **Transparency** | Clearly communicate how data is collected and used. |
| ⚖️ **Fairness** | Avoid unfair treatment or discriminatory outcomes. |
| 🛡️ **Security** | Protect data from unauthorized access or misuse. |
| 🤝 **Accountability** | Organizations should take responsibility for their data practices. |
| 🧑‍⚖️ **Respect for Individuals** | Consider the rights and interests of people affected by data processing. |

# ⚖️ 1. Balance Between Privacy and Security

Privacy and security are closely related but are not identical concepts.

**Security** focuses on protecting information and systems from unauthorized access, modification, disclosure, or destruction.

**Privacy** focuses on how personal information is collected, used, shared, and retained.

Security measures can help protect privacy, but security decisions can also raise privacy questions.

### Example

Consider a university that wants to improve campus security by installing surveillance cameras.

### Possible Benefits

- Helps investigate security incidents.
- May discourage unauthorized activities.
- Can assist in protecting students and staff.

### Possible Privacy Concerns

- Continuous monitoring of individuals.
- Collection of information about people's movements.
- Storage and retention of surveillance footage.
- Unauthorized access or misuse of recordings.
- Possible use of collected information for purposes beyond security.

### Ethical Approach

Before implementing such a system, an organization should consider:

1. Is the surveillance necessary?
2. Is the purpose clearly defined?
3. Is the amount of data collected reasonable?
4. Who can access the information?
5. How long will the information be retained?
6. Are individuals appropriately informed?
7. Are there less intrusive alternatives?

This demonstrates that security measures should be designed with privacy considerations in mind.

# 👥 2. Impact on Marginalized Communities

Data collection and automated analysis can have different effects on different groups of people.

Marginalized communities may face additional risks when datasets are incomplete, biased, or collected without sufficient consideration of their circumstances.

## ⚠️ Potential Concerns

### A. Underrepresentation

If a group is poorly represented in a dataset, systems developed using that dataset may perform less effectively for members of that group.

### B. Historical Bias

Data may reflect existing social or institutional inequalities. Using such data without proper analysis can reproduce or reinforce those patterns.

### C. Discriminatory Outcomes

Automated systems may produce outcomes that disproportionately disadvantage certain groups.

### D. Lack of Participation

People affected by data-driven systems may not have meaningful opportunities to understand or challenge decisions involving their information.

### E. Privacy Risks

Sensitive information about vulnerable groups can create additional risks if improperly disclosed or misused.
